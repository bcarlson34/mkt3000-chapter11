Discussion challenges

Challenge 1: Hold everything constant. Compare direct, retail, and wholesale structures. Which member gains margin? How do prices, demand, and overall channel contribution change?

Challenge 2: Switch from economy to express shipping. Do incremental sales outweigh the extra shipping cost?

Challenge 3: Add buffer inventory. Is preventing expected stockouts worth $1 per unit fulfilled?

Challenge 4: Turn on the placement allowance. Why can the producer and retailer contributions change even if total channel contribution does not?

Challenge 5: Who pays shipping? Contrast the customer's all-in price with the seller's profit.

Assumptions & limitations

  • One order contains one unit. Shipping cost is per order/unit, with no returns or volume discounts.
  • Each channel member's markup is applied sequentially to its product acquisition cost. Intermediate markups do not include shipping or allowance transfers.
  • Wholesalers incur $0.50 handling cost per unit fulfilled. No other intermediate distribution fees are modeled.
  • When shipping is free at checkout, the last channel member absorbs the carrier cost. When shipping is paid, the customer pays the carrier and it is included in delivered price.
  • Buffer inventory costs the last channel member $1 per fulfilled unit; its assumed benefits apply only through lower expected stockouts. Real carrying costs also depend on unsold inventory and time.
  • The producer pays the placement allowance to the retailer; the retailer receives the same amount. It is a transfer within the channel, so the amount cancels in total channel contribution.
  • Demand effects of channel reach, retail convenience, promotion, service, competitor actions and repeat purchases are held constant. This simplified model does not prove which channel is best in the real world.
